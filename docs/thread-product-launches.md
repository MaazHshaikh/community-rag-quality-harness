# Big launch day or quiet launch?

**Doc type:** Discussion thread
**Date:** 2026-08-05

**Theo Adeyemi** (founder, Patchwell, bug tracking for game studios):
We're about six weeks from opening up publicly. I keep hearing two schools of thought: plan one big coordinated launch day, or release quietly and grow from there. Which did you do and would you do it again?

**Ingrid Solberg** (founder, Glasswing, accessibility audits for websites):
Big launch, no question. We spent four weeks preparing: a waitlist of 2,300 people, a launch video, 15 friendly founders ready to share on the day, and posts on three launch sites at once. We got 640 signups in 48 hours and two inbound investor emails. You only get one first impression, so make it loud.

**Mateo Rossi** (founder, Saltmarsh, CRM for fishing co-ops):
I'd do the opposite. A big launch brings a spike of curious people who don't match your customer profile. We did a loud launch in year one and 80% of those signups never came back after the first week. For our relaunch we opened access to 20 users per week, fixed what broke, and only then announced publicly. Retention went from 12% to 41% at 30 days.

**Ingrid Solberg:**
Mateo, the spike also brings press and investors, which a quiet launch never will. You can filter the users later.

**Mateo Rossi:**
You can, but your first support queue gets buried in noise from people who will never pay. With a small team that hurts.

**Priscilla Ann Moore** (founder, Hollowbrook, scheduling for therapy practices):
I think the answer depends on whether your product is ready. My rule: don't do a big launch until at least 10 users are using the product every week without you prompting them. Before that, a big launch just shows a lot of people a product that isn't ready.

**Theo Adeyemi:**
That's a useful test. We have 7 weekly active users right now, so by that rule I should run a few more quiet weeks first.
