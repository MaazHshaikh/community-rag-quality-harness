# Raise pre-seed now, or wait for revenue?

**Doc type:** Discussion thread
**Date:** 2026-07-14

**Ravi Menon** (founder, Docklight, compliance tool for small logistics firms):
We have a working prototype, 6 pilot customers, and $0 revenue. An angel offered $150K on a post-money SAFE with a $4M cap. Part of me wants to take it now. Part of me thinks we should wait until we have real revenue and raise on better terms. What would you do?

**Claire Dubois** (founder, Mapleweave, hiring software for clinics):
Take it. Raise as early as you can. I raised $400K pre-revenue on a $5M cap, and that money let me hire an engineer six months sooner than I could have on my own. Speed matters more than dilution at this stage. Giving up 3 or 4 extra percent is nothing compared to losing a year.

**Jonah Whitfield** (founder, Quartzline, bookkeeping for dental offices):
I strongly disagree. Don't raise until you hit at least $10K in monthly recurring revenue. I bootstrapped to $12K MRR before raising anything, and I closed my round in three weeks at a $9M cap. Investors compete when you have revenue. Without it, you're selling a story, and you'll pay for that in dilution and in pressure to grow before you're ready.

**Claire Dubois:**
Jonah, that works if you can afford to go a year without a salary. Most founders can't. Waiting for $10K MRR filters out anyone without savings.

**Jonah Whitfield:**
Fair point on savings, but early money also creates expectations. My cofounder at my last company raised early, burned through it in 14 months, and we had to shut down. Revenue first keeps you honest.

**Amara Nwosu** (founder, Fieldnote, research tool for agronomists):
Both of you are describing your own situations. Ravi, a $4M cap for $150K is 3.75% dilution. The real question is whether $150K gets you to a milestone that makes the next raise easier, such as $5K MRR or 20 paying customers. If yes, take it. If it just buys time without a clear goal, wait.

**Ravi Menon:**
Thanks, all. I'll map out what $150K buys us before deciding, and I'll book a fundraising office hours slot with Marcus.
