# Who should your first hire be?

**Doc type:** Discussion thread
**Date:** 2026-05-30

**Nadia Haddad** (founder, Tidecount, water usage monitoring for farms):
I'm a solo non-technical founder with $250K raised. I can afford one full-time hire for about 18 months. Should my first hire be an engineer, a salesperson, or a generalist who can do a bit of everything?

**Felix Brandt** (founder, Copperstep, field service scheduling):
Hire a generalist. My first hire was someone who could write basic code, run customer calls, and handle support. In the first year your needs change every month, and a specialist gets bored or blocked when their specialty isn't the priority. My generalist is still with us three years later and now runs operations.

**Yuki Tanaka** (founder, Brineworks, lab software for food safety testing):
I'd push back on that. Hire the strongest specialist you can find in the area you're weakest at. You're non-technical, Nadia, so hire a senior engineer. I hired a generalist first and we shipped a product that half worked. When I finally hired a senior engineer in month 14, she rebuilt most of it in four months. I lost a year.

**Felix Brandt:**
Yuki, a senior engineer will cost Nadia at least $150K a year. That uses most of her runway on one person, and then who sells?

**Yuki Tanaka:**
Nadia can sell. Founders should do all the selling until about $1M in annual revenue anyway. What she can't do is build.

**Marisol Ortega** (founder, Pebblecart, ordering platform for school cafeterias):
One thing both of you skipped: whoever you hire, use a 90-day trial with clear goals written down on day one. My first hire looked great in interviews and wasn't a fit. Because we had written goals, the conversation at day 90 was easy. Also, give early hires real equity. I offered 1.5% to my first employee with a 4-year vesting schedule and a 1-year cliff, which is pretty standard.

**Nadia Haddad:**
Thank you. I'm leaning toward an engineer because building is the thing I truly can't do, but I'll set the 90-day goals no matter who I hire.
