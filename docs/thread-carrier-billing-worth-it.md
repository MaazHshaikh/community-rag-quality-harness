# Is direct carrier billing worth it?

**Doc type:** Discussion thread
**Date:** 2026-08-12

**Zara Sheikh** (founder, Pixelpond, casual mobile games):
We sell in-game coins in Pakistan. Many of our players don't have cards. ZoisPay offers direct carrier billing in Pakistan, Kenya, and the UAE, but the fee is 12% compared with 2.9% plus 30 cents for domestic cards. Is it worth it?

**Omar Farouk** (growth lead, Tunebox, music streaming):
For us it was the best decision we made. Most of our listeners in Pakistan have no card at all, so the real comparison isn't 12% versus 2.9%. It's 12% versus zero sales. After adding carrier billing, paid conversions in Pakistan nearly tripled. Payment is two taps and a confirmation code, with no form to fill in.

**Rebecca Williams** (finance lead, Storyhatch, e-books):
I'd be careful. The 12% fee is only part of the cost. Carrier billing settles after 30 days, while cards settle after 2 business days, so it ties up a lot of cash. Refunds are only possible within 7 days and only in full. As a digital-goods seller on carrier billing, you're also in a high-risk category with a 15% reserve held for 120 days. We switched it off after one quarter because our cash flow couldn't take it.

**Omar Farouk:**
Rebecca, the settlement delay is real, but it's a cash-flow problem, not a profit problem. If the customers can't pay any other way, the delay is the price of having them at all.

**Rebecca Williams:**
That's true if you have the cash to wait. Many small studios don't.

**Kevin Otieno** (developer, Mtaa Games, puzzle games in Kenya):
I think it depends on your price point. Carrier billing is capped at $50 per payment, and in our experience it works best under $5, where customers buy on impulse. For larger purchases, we push mobile wallets instead: 2.2% plus 20 cents, and they settle the next business day.

**Zara Sheikh:**
Great points. Our coin packs are $1 to $4, so we'll try carrier billing in Pakistan only and plan for the 30-day settlement before expanding.
