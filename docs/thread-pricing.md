# Free tier or charge from day one?

**Doc type:** Discussion thread
**Date:** 2026-07-28

**Samir Qureshi** (founder, Inkfold, contract management for small agencies):
Our beta is ending next month. I'm torn between launching with a free tier to get adoption, or charging everyone from the first day. Current plan is $49 per month per team. What has worked for you?

**Beatriz Lopes** (founder, Northwick, payroll for restaurants):
Charge from day one, and don't offer a free tier. Free users give you misleading feedback because they have nothing at stake. When we switched from free to $79 per month, we lost 70% of our users, but the ones who stayed told us exactly what they needed. Paying customers are the only real signal.

**Kofi Mensah** (founder, Stackhollow, team wikis for remote startups):
This depends heavily on your market. For us, a free tier was essential. Our free plan allows up to 5 users, and about 6% of free teams upgrade to paid within 6 months. More than half of our paying customers started on the free plan. Without it we'd have a fraction of the growth.

**Beatriz Lopes:**
Kofi, a wiki spreads through teams on its own. Samir sells to agencies that make a buying decision once. A free tier there just attracts people who will never pay.

**Kofi Mensah:**
That's fair for a top-down sale. But I'd still never put a hard paywall in front of a product people haven't tried. At minimum, offer a 14-day free trial.

**Lucia Ferrante** (founder, Driftmark, analytics for podcasters):
One thing I learned: whatever you choose, price higher than feels comfortable. We started at $19 per month and nobody complained, which meant we were too cheap. We raised the price to $39 and lost only 4 of 120 customers. Founders almost always underprice.

**Samir Qureshi:**
So the advice splits on free versus paid, but everyone seems to agree I shouldn't go lower than planned. I'll launch at $49 with a 14-day trial and no permanent free tier, then revisit after three months.
