# How did you get your first 100 customers?

**Doc type:** Discussion thread
**Date:** 2026-06-22

**Hana Okafor** (founder, Ledgerpine, invoicing for freelancers):
We launched three months ago and have 31 paying customers. Ads have been a waste so far: we spent $1,200 and got 4 signups. For those of you who passed 100 customers, what actually worked?

**Diego Marquez** (founder, Shelfwright, inventory tool for small bookstores):
Cold email, done by hand. I worked in rounds of 350 to 400 independent bookstores at a time and wrote each owner a short, personal note. Over five months I sent 9 rounds, roughly 3,400 emails in total. About 9% replied and roughly a third of those became customers, which got me to 100. The key was writing every email myself. When I tried a template tool, replies dropped to 2%.

**Lena Vasquez** (founder, Crumbtrail, analytics for bakeries):
Go where your customers already gather. I joined three regional baking associations and gave a free 20-minute talk at each of their meetups. Each talk brought in 10 to 15 customers. Online ads never beat that for us. It's slow and doesn't scale, but your first 100 customers don't need to scale.

**Oskar Lindgren** (founder, Pondly, scheduling for swim schools):
I'll add one warning. My first 40 customers came from friends and my old network. They were polite, but they didn't give honest feedback and half of them churned within 90 days. Customers 41 to 100 came from strangers through a partnership with a swim-school software reseller, and they were far more useful. Don't count friends as real validation.

**Hana Okafor:**
This is really helpful. Sounds like the common thread is manual, personal outreach to a narrow group. I'll pause the ads and try Diego's approach with 200 freelancer agencies.

**Diego Marquez:**
One more tip: track every email in a spreadsheet with the date, reply, and outcome. After 100 emails you'll see which subject lines and which customer types respond, and you can stop wasting time on the rest.
